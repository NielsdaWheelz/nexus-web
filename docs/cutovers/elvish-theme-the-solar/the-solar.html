§1 · The story

Three rooms of one house, ascending

A solar is the real architectural name for the withdrawing room built above a great hall — the private upper chamber, glazed on the west side, where the household read and wrote and kept its books while the hall below stayed loud. It is the one room in the building designed around late light. The Press is the night workshop below. The Study is the day desk on the main floor. The Solar is the room you climb to when you want to be alone with a long text and not be reachable.

The sun has gone behind the valley wall, the west glazing is full of the canopy, and the green light coming through the leaves is the only light in the room except one brass lamp and, later, the stars. Nothing is dramatic. Everything is old, made by hand, and unhurried.

The elvish register is not a costume over this; it is the room's logic. Things that grow are open, asymmetric and branching, never knotted or closed. Metal is aged, never new. The writing on the walls is in a hand you are not asked to read, only to feel the discipline of. And there are exactly two lights, which never trade jobs:

The lamp · interaction

Warm brass, #c9a95f. Hover, links, selection, the pressed key. Light with a source and a direction — it falls from the leading edge, it never uniformly brightens a box.

The moon · attention

Cool silver, #cfe6e0. Focus rings, the lit pane, the Ithildin reveal. One warm, one cool, four steps of lightness apart — distinguishable with no hue perception at all.

§2–3 · The materials

Twilight green — a third pole, not a tint

The Study is paper and the Press is ink; a third room that is merely "dark with a green tint" is a skin, not a room. The Solar's canvas carries roughly 2.5× the light of the Press's near-black, and it carries chroma where both other rooms carry none — air with light inside it. The decisive call was hue: warm olive-walnut was rejected because the Press is already aged gold on warm black, and the third room's whole job is to be a third thing. Green-grey, hue 150–160°, with chroma rising with lightness — pushing chroma up at low lightness is exactly what turns dark greens to murk.

§5 · The script

Tengwar with zero font bytes

Tengwar webfonts are transliteration fonts: bind one to a live DOM string and you get glyph-soup — the single most fan-recognisable failure available. So no Tengwar font ever ships. Every inscription is composed by hand, transcribed independently in two tools (Glaemscribe and Tecendil), diffed — if the tools disagree, the line is wrong and gets rewritten, never fudged — then set, kerned, converted to outlines, and frozen as SVG paths with its language, mode, translation and provenance recorded. It appears in exactly five places: the divider band, the reader's chapter opener, empty states, rule terminals, and the colophon where the full inscription is shown with its transcription and translation — if we are going to do it, we show our work.

The band: cropped stems and bows used as pure repeating geometry, the way a Greek key is geometry, faded at both ends so it is visibly an open band and not a sentence. Nothing is meant to be read, so nothing can be misread. (The forms above are abstract by design — real inscriptions only ever ship double-tool-verified.)

Where it never appears: numerals, buttons, labels, tabs, menus, tooltips, toasts, errors, counts, timestamps — any string a user must parse or act on. Numerals stay Arabic and tabular, always. A lint gate enforces that no Tengwar font-family exists anywhere in the bundle.

§6–7 · Light & motion

Ithildin — ornament that answers attention

The room's signature, used on exactly two element types. At rest the pane-header vine is drawn in --edge-subtle — present, perceptually silent. When the pane holds your attention (:focus-within, not a mouse passing through) it silvers and blooms over 220ms. Exit is instant: in at 220ms, out at 80ms. Ornament that lingers on the way out reads as lag.

§6 · The ornament system

One motif, three cuts, one dimmer

Everything ornamental in the room is a single-stroke open branch — asymmetric, growing out of an edge toward the margin, three leaves maximum, terminating off-frame, never closing on itself. It ships as three cuts of one asset: a seamless 240×12 vine tile, a 48×48 corner branch, a 10×10 leaf terminal — all applied as mask-image over token gradients, so every ornament recolors from the theme for free. And every ornament multiplies its opacity by one number, --ornament: set it to 0 and the whole system goes silent without moving a pixel.

Illuminated

--ornament: 1

Empty states, the reader's chapter opener, Settings → Appearance, the colophon. Fewer than eight surfaces in the entire app; at most three ornaments per screen.

Whisper

--ornament: .5

Pane header rule, section openers, sheet top edge, player progress, scrollbar, focus ring. One mark, one edge — nothing that costs a repaint.

Bare

--ornament: 0

Every list row, table, form, chat message, reading column, dense grid, virtualized list. Color and hairline only. Dense views drop here automatically.

The test: the Solar with all ornament stripped is still a beautiful green room. If that is not true, the palette is doing too little and the vines are doing too much.

Nothing here is lost

An empty shelf in the Solar is a place, not an error.

The illuminated tier, where ornament is the content: corner branches, a Cormorant headline, and the six-pointed star of Eärendil — the wayfinder. Six points, not eight: Fëanor's eight-ray star is a craft guild's badge, the wrong connotation for a knowledge instrument. In the app, a verified Tengwar line sits above the English.

§10 · The wildcards

Three strange things, all real

Living marginalia

The reader's margin rail carries one continuous vine spanning the document — simultaneously the progress bar, the highlight map, and the page's illumination. The stroke draws only as far as your furthest-read position, so the vine grows as you read the book. Every highlight sprouts a leaf tinted by its ink; hovering a leaf scrubs to it. Your current position is a brass bud. And the wood remembers where you walk: long-dwell positions leave a permanent, almost imperceptible worn mark — reopen a book you have read three times and the rail carries a faint path, brighter where you stopped and re-read, blank across the chapters you skimmed.

Three widgets collapsed into one drawing; the drawing is different for every book because you made it — the groove worn into a wooden step by people going up to read.

The Ithildin obeys the actual moon

Ithildin can only be seen by moonlight — so a dozen lines of client-side arithmetic compute the real lunar phase and scale the reveal's brightness from new moon to full. By rule, the moon scalar may only ever touch an opacity, a glow radius, or a hue slot — never a lightness or chroma any text sits on — so no phase of the moon can move a single contrast ratio. Nobody is told. A person who reads daily for a month will one evening notice the app has been quietly keeping a calendar.

tonight's moon: …

The room outside time

The star field in the grain is seeded once per session — a different sky each time you arrive, then perfectly still for as long as you stay. No drift, no day/night shift, no greeting, no clock. Every other room in the app can tell you what time it is; the Solar refuses. Its rejected rival was an hour-drifting canvas — beautiful engineering, wrong room: a canvas that changes while you read is a room that fidgets, and the Solar is the place you climb to in order to be unreachable.

§9 · The refusals

What this room will never do

No closed interlaceKnots, braids, medallions and wreaths read Dwarvish and generic-fantasy. Every ornament is open, botanical, and exits the frame.
No ChristmasGreens desaturated toward moss; gold aged to brass; red pulled to coral; the pairs never adjacent at full strength.
No casinoNo looping shimmer, shine sweeps, glitter or particles. One glow exists, it answers attention, and it stops.
No Skyrim modNo bevels, hammered metal, carved stone, parchment panels or scroll-shaped buttons. Flat confident color; the only "material" is 5% grain, a 1px rim light, and one conic gradient reserved for actual metal.
No fantasy serif at body sizeCormorant never renders below 20px. Inter carries every functional string.
No fake runesDouble-tool-verified baked inscriptions or abstract cropped geometry. Nothing in between; no Tengwar webfont in the bundle.
No Tengwar that means anythingIf deleting it loses information, it is a bug.
No ornament on contentNo gradient text, vines, tints or grain inside reading text, chat answers, or code. Ornament lives on edges and in absences.
No ornament in dense viewsPast ~12 visible rows, the dimmer goes to zero. Ornament that is everywhere is noise.
No hour-drifting canvasThe room outside time won the arbitration.
No touching identityFavicon, icons, OG image and manifest stay brand-dark. The Solar is a room inside the product, not the product.
No meaning carried by ornament aloneEvery vine, glow and glyph decorates a state already legible in color, position and text.

The tournament

Three concepts entered; one room came out

Three designers worked blind from the same research: Rivendell (autumn brass on olive-walnut), Lothlórien (green twilight under starlight), and an unconstrained Visionary (an hour-drifting conservatory). The judge scored them on weighted criteria; Lothlórien won on doctrine — a genuine third pole, two lights that never trade jobs, the only zero-risk Tengwar policy — then took Rivendell's name, its contrast rigor, and its Living Marginalia, and the Visionary's contrast-invariance proof, pointed at the one element with a canonical right to respond to time.

The build

Four slices, each lovely on its own

The blueprint is verified file-by-file against main @ 42f33dc4fc — a full-app theme like light and dark (not oracle-scoped), color-scheme: dark, a cookie union widened to three, and zero token-gate exemptions: the proof the room has no seams.

S0

The core token room

Cookie union, the [data-theme="elvish"] block, grain tile, gilt selection, brass scrollbar, the settings option, the dossier-frame mapping, the widened contrast test.

Pick "Solar" in Settings and every surface in the app is green dusk with brass, instantly, no reload, no flash — and the token gate passes with no new exemption.
S1

Typography — the room gets a voice

EB Garamond becomes the reading serif at zero new bytes; Cormorant 300 arrives as two woff2 files (~40KB) for display sizes only.

Open a long text: real Garamond body, Cormorant pane titles, nothing below 20px — and the other two rooms byte-identical.
S2

The ornament system

Vine, corner, leaf and band as masked assets; the Ithildin pane rule; row leaf + lamp glow; the growing search underline; the dimmer and its automatic degradation.

Nine panes open — exactly one silvered rule. Set the dimmer to zero in devtools: still a beautiful green room, nothing moved a pixel, 500-row lists at 60fps.
S3

Signature moments & wildcards

Living marginalia in the margin rail, the three-doorway settings pane, the chapter opener, the colophon, verified inscriptions, the lunar scalar with its unit-tested slot rule.

Reopen a book you've read — the vine is drawn as far as you got, leaves at your highlights, a worn path where you dwell. On a full moon the rules are brighter, and no contrast ratio has moved.

Chief landmines, already mapped: the sealed dossier iframe would render white for an OS-light user (one-line fix, S0); a cookie-readback regex in the settings pane that silently reverts the theme if forgotten; and mask-image has zero prior use in the codebase with Chromium-only CI — Firefox and WebKit get checked by eye.
