# Elvish Theme — Surface Map & Design Language Research

## 1. Design principles (from the docs, not invented)

**docs/chapbook.md** (the mobile/product thesis) is the deepest and most binding source. Its "Art direction: the quiet press" section (§ "Art direction") is the house visual constitution:

- The visual goal is explicitly **not** "AI app," "productivity dashboard," or "glass UI." It is *"a beautifully made edition that happens to be alive."*
- **Materials before decoration**: hairlines, spacing, and typography come before cards and shadows; two or three elevations at most; translucency only when it preserves orientation.
- **Ink roles are explicit and few**: canvas / surface / ink / muted ink / hairline / link-accent / machine-ink / danger / focus-ring / highlight-set — a small closed vocabulary, not an open palette.
- **Shape law**: avoid nesting rounded rectangles inside rounded rectangles; capsules reserved for compact actions/state; lists read as *editorial rhythm, not a pile of tiles*; media artwork may break the typographic field, decoration may not.
- **Character**: "futuristic without being loud" — the magic is continuity and intelligence, not visual effects. Concrete examples given: a selection that physically becomes a note citation, a search result that opens on the exact sentence — i.e., causal, earned motion, not ornament for its own sake.
- **Human and machine voices are typographically separated** (see Machine Hand below) — this is the precedent for how a *third* register (elvish) could sit alongside human and machine registers without fighting either.
- **Interaction laws** (§ "Interaction laws"): causal motion, interruptible motion, direct manipulation, brief motion, spatial consistency, reduced-motion preserves meaning via fades not removal, haptics mark commitments not every tap.
- **The artifact stays sovereign; tools arrive, help, and recede** — the mission-critical refrain repeated at the top of the whole document and echoed in "AI waits at the edge of the desk." Any elvish theme ornament (borders, Tengwar accents, glyphs) must behave like a tool: present, legible as decoration, never intercepting reading focus. This is the single strongest constraint against over-decorating body text or reading surfaces.
- **Surface recipes** (§ "Surface recipes") give per-surface material rules already: root list = canvas + typographic section heads + hairline/whitespace, almost no cards; reader = uninterrupted canvas, chrome on one quiet material; sheet = one lifted surface, 20pt top corners; note = page typography with citations as marginal chips/rails, not pills; machine output = readable prose + cool attribution rail + colophon.

**docs/horizons.md / dreams.md / scriptorium.md** — a trilogy of "what next" vision docs, not visual-system docs, but they name two design lineages directly relevant to a third room:
- **dreams.md, "Act I — the Machine Hand"**: names the *manuscript register* explicitly — "Oracle typography escapes the Oracle — the manuscript register (`[data-theme='oracle']`, EB Garamond, marginalia) applied to generated artifacts app-wide" was a convergent idea across independent design lenses. This is direct precedent/appetite for a **named alternate typographic room outgrowing its original scoped niche** — exactly the move an elvish theme would make (a fully worked scoped room, not a bolt-on skin).
- **scriptorium.md**: "the working-house thesis" — frames the whole app as a **press/scriptorium metaphor** (rooms you work in, apparatus/marginalia engines, a "house agent," a "night shift"). This vocabulary (press, room, apparatus, scriptorium, colophon) is the house's own idiom for "distinct, well-made publishing environments," which an elvish "Greenwood Press" or similar third room slots into naturally.

**docs/local-rules/codebase.md / tech-stack.md**: pure engineering ownership rules (imports, module boundaries, Bun toolchain) — no visual-design content, but confirm the token-discipline gate (`check-css-tokens.mjs`) and the "one owner per concern" ethic that the Two Rooms and Machine Hand cutovers already follow. Any new theme should follow the same discipline: scoped tokens, one guard test, no silent forks.

## 2–3. Surface inventory + where elvish could uniquely live

### The Nexus switchboard (desktop palette / mobile command list)
- Owner: `apps/web/src/components/switchboard/` — `switchboard.module.css` (492 lines), `SwitchboardSearch.tsx`, `SwitchboardRow.tsx`, `SwitchboardActions.tsx`, `SwitchboardTask.tsx`, `SwitchboardRecovery.tsx`.
- Current signature: sticky header on `--surface-canvas`, `text-lg` heading with `tracking-tight`, icon/text buttons, a vertical row list (mobile hard-cut to a vertical command list per recent commits #202/#205 — no more carousel/swipe grid).
- Elvish opportunities: (1) the sticky header rule/divider under the search field — a Tengwar-inspired hairline flourish instead of a flat rule; (2) row hover/active state — a leaf-vein or branch-curve background wash instead of flat `--surface-hover`; (3) the section labels (small-caps in Study/Press) could carry an elvish-gold rule-and-glyph treatment as a section opener, echoing "running-journal" section openers mentioned in the Two Rooms doc.

### Pane system (headers, chrome, instrument rows)
- Owner: `apps/web/src/components/ui/PaneHeaderIdentity.tsx` / `.module.css`, `apps/web/src/lib/panes/paneHeaderModel.ts`.
- Current signature: two-line identity block (title + support/credit line), `text-base`/`weight-semibold` title, `text-xs`/`ink-muted` support line with inline credit chips — deliberately minimal, no card, no icon badge by default.
- Elvish opportunities: (1) the divider/border below the pane header — a curved or leaf-notched rule instead of a straight hairline (a bounded, cheap CSS `border-image` or subtle SVG background rather than a redesign of the component); (2) a small elvish glyph as the *machine-provenance* marker equivalent — but only if it stays inside the existing MachineText-style quarantine, never as a generic pane icon; (3) focus ring — `--ring` token could adopt a colored, slightly organic double-ring in elvish rather than a hard rectangle, still meeting the "independent high-contrast token" accessibility law.

### Reader (HTML/PDF/EPUB — margins, highlights, selection toolbar)
- Owner: `apps/web/src/components/reader/` — `MarginRail.module.css`, `ReaderDocumentMapOverviewRail.module.css`, `MobileReaderPositionRibbon.module.css`, `ReaderContentsNav.module.css`, `textDocumentReader.module.css`, plus `.readerThemeLight`/`.readerThemeDark` in `settings/reader/page.module.css:337–364` — explicitly **hardcoded and independent of the app theme**, documented at `page.module.css:6–11` as intentionally not migrated to semantic tokens.
- This is the single most important constraint for elvish: the reader's own theme (light/dark) is a *separate* system from the app room theme. An elvish reader theme, if ever built, would need its own `.readerThemeElvish` block — it does not inherit automatically from `[data-theme="elvish"]` the way most of the app does. This mirrors how Two Rooms had to explicitly document that its body-level tokens "compose, do not fight" with reader tokens (§4.5 of that cutover) rather than assuming inheritance.
- Elvish opportunities, scoped to *chrome only* (never reading text, honoring "artifact stays sovereign"): (1) `MarginRail`/`ReaderContentsNav` — vine/branch-curve dividers between chapter entries instead of straight rules; (2) drop caps at chapter openers — a purely typographic, high-craft opportunity mentioned nowhere yet in the codebase but consistent with "source-authored hierarchy is retained when it helps comprehension" (chapbook §"Text system") — an elvish drop-cap treatment (illuminated-manuscript-adjacent, using a decorative border rather than Tengwar glyphs since Tengwar is unreadable for core content) could be a genuinely novel, tasteful addition; (3) selection/highlight toolbar accent color — the four-action bar (`Highlight · Note · Link · Ask`) could use elvish gold/green accent tokens without altering its geometry, since it's already accent-token-driven.

### Library lists (Editorial ResourceRow)
- Owner: `apps/web/src/components/ui/ResourceRow.tsx` / `.module.css`.
- Current signature: CSS-grid row, `border-bottom: var(--stroke-hairline) solid var(--edge-subtle)` (this literally forks 1px Study / 1.5px Press per the Two Rooms table), hover/focus-within → `--surface-1`, selected → `--accent-muted`. No cards, no rounded tiles — matches the explicit "editorial, type-forward" house taste and the memory note rejecting generic rounded-card design.
- Elvish opportunities: (1) `--stroke-hairline` is *already* a per-room forked token — an elvish room gets this for free just by defining its own value (a slightly warmer, leaf-green hairline, or a very subtle dashed/notched rule reminiscent of vellum ruling); (2) `.selected` background (`--accent-muted`) — elvish gold-green wash; (3) row hover state could carry the single most "extraordinary, not generic" opportunity in the whole app per the memory note on list design — an elvish row hover could reveal a barely-visible leaf/vine motif at the row's leading edge, similar in spirit to how media artwork "may break the typographic field" per the shape law.

### The Launcher / command surface
- No component literally named "Launcher" survives in the current tree (superseded/renamed) — the live equivalent is the **switchboard** (§ above). Treat "Launcher" and "switchboard" as the same surface for elvish purposes; do not invent a second component.

### Chat / conversation
- Governed by `docs/cutovers/chat-interface-hard-cutover.md` (not deep-read this pass) and explicitly **excluded** from Machine Hand register (assistant prose stays in normal sans, not machine-ink). Elvish opportunities are therefore chrome-only: composer border, Stop/Retry button styling, Sources/Details disclosure rule — never the answer prose itself, which chapbook explicitly protects ("no machine edit that masquerades as human writing" cuts both ways — no room theme should make chat prose look decoratively "elvish" either).

### The oracle route (existing themed precedent)
- Owner: `[data-theme="oracle"]` block in `apps/web/src/app/globals.css:284–296`, applied by `OracleThemeWrapper.tsx` — a one-line `<div data-theme="oracle" style={{display:"contents"}}>` wrapper, scoped to `/oracle/**`.
- Its tokens: `--oracle-bg #14110f`, `--oracle-bg-elevated #1c1814`, `--oracle-fg #ece1c8`, `--oracle-fg-muted #b6a482`, `--oracle-rule #4a3b2a`, `--oracle-gold #c39a4d`, `--oracle-cream #f3e9d0`, `--oracle-maroon #6b2a2a`, `--oracle-marginalia-fg #8e7848`, plus three dedicated font stacks: `--font-oracle-body` (EB Garamond), `--font-oracle-display` (IM Fell English), `--font-oracle-fraktur` (UniFraktur Maguntia) — all self-hosted local woff2 already in `apps/web/src/app/fonts/`.
- Look/feel: "Black Forest" manuscript — near-black warm ground, aged-parchment cream text, muted gold accent, maroon as a secondary accent, a dedicated marginalia-fg color distinct from body fg, Gothic/Fraktur available for display moments, italic serif (EB Garamond/IM Fell) for body/display. This is the closest sibling to what elvish should be architecturally: a **fully separate, non-inheriting, route-or-flag-scoped token namespace** (`--oracle-*` prefix) that does not touch `--surface-*`/`--ink-*` at all, applied via a trivial wrapper `div`, rather than overriding the semantic tokens app-wide the way Study/Press do. Elvish has a real choice here: be a third *global room* (fork the semantic tokens like Two Rooms) or be a *scoped alternate persona* like Oracle. The docs don't dictate which; it's a genuine open design decision for the design-brief phase.
- Two Rooms' AC-11 also proves the two systems are designed to compose safely: oracle content sits *above* the canvas-grain layer (z-index -1, occluded by oracle's own opaque background), and body-level letter-spacing/font-size tokens don't leak into oracle because oracle doesn't define or consume them. Any elvish global-room design must preserve this same non-interference contract with Oracle.

### Settings panes
- Owner: `apps/web/src/app/(authenticated)/settings/appearance/SettingsAppearancePaneBody.tsx` (renders Study/Press/System radio options with hint text) + `SettingsPaneBody.tsx` (top-level description string). This is the **only UI surface a new theme must touch to become user-selectable** — precedent from Two Rooms shows the pattern is a third radio option with its own label + one-line hint (e.g. "Elvish — Greenwood canopy, gold and green.").
- No component redesign needed; elvish just needs a slot in this existing three-option (soon four) list, plus (if global-room) a third value alongside `light`/`dark` in the appearance cookie type, or (if scoped-persona like Oracle) no settings change at all — just a route or a per-user flag.

### Player / Lectern
- Owner: `apps/web/src/components/player/PlayerControls.module.css`, `PlayerPlaybackControls.module.css`, `MobileMiniPlayer.module.css`, `GlobalPlayerSurfaces.module.css`, plus `apps/web/src/app/(authenticated)/lectern/LecternPaneBody.module.css` and `LecternMutationNotice/NextPrompt.module.css`.
- Not deep-inspected this pass, but per chapbook's Now Playing spec (§"Listening and watching"), this surface is transport-first and artwork-first; elvish opportunities are confined to progress-bar fill color, transport button accent, and the MiniPlayer's expansion affordance — never artwork or the transport layout itself ("controls remain in the global ink system rather than sampling an unreadable theme" — chapbook, Surface recipes → Media).

### Empty states and loading states
- No single owning component found (`EmptyState` appears used ad hoc, e.g. in `LibraryChooser.tsx`); these are the single highest-leverage, lowest-risk canvas for elvish illustration — empty states are inherently decorative real estate that doesn't compete with content. A restrained line-art vine/branch motif or a single Tengwar-inspired ornamental rule under the empty-state message would be tasteful and low-risk, consistent with "media artwork can break the typographic field, but decoration cannot" (i.e., decoration must stay out of *content* areas, but an empty state's entire content *is* the decoration slot).

### Toasts / dialogs / sheets
- Owner: `apps/web/src/components/ui/Dialog.module.css`, `MobileSheet.module.css`, plus feature-specific dialogs (`LibrarySettingsDialog.module.css`, `LinkTargetDialog.module.css`, `PodcastSubscriptionSettingsDialog.module.css`).
- Per chapbook's Surface recipes: "Sheet: one lifted surface, 20-point top corners, visible task title and close control, no cards nested merely to create depth." Elvish opportunities: sheet top-corner treatment (the existing 20pt radius could gain a subtle carved/organic corner detail at very low visual weight), and the scrim color (currently presumably a neutral dark) could carry the faintest green-gold cast in the elvish room only.

## 4. Brand assets — what exists, whether a theme should touch identity

- `apps/web/src/app/brand.css` — **auto-generated** by `apps/web/scripts/build-icons.mjs` ("Do not edit by hand"). Defines exactly four tokens: `--brand-fg-on-dark #c4a472`, `--brand-fg-on-light #7d5e35`, `--brand-bg-dark #15140f`, `--brand-bg-light #faf8f3`. These are the literal source values that `--accent`/`--accent`-family tokens in globals.css reference (`--accent: var(--brand-fg-on-light)` in Study, `var(--brand-fg-on-dark)` in Press) — i.e. **brand identity and the Study/Press accent are the same four numbers**, generated once and consumed everywhere.
- `apps/web/src/app/icon.svg` — the app's favicon/PWA icon: a three-dot "asterism" mark (`ASTERISM_DOTS`, `ASTERISM_VIEWBOX` — referenced from `@/lib/brand`, not read this pass but clearly a shared geometry constant), color-switches via `prefers-color-scheme` between `--brand-fg-on-light`/`-on-dark` directly in inline SVG `<style>` (not CSS custom properties, since favicons render outside the app's DOM/theme context).
- `apps/web/src/app/apple-icon.tsx` and `apps/web/src/app/opengraph-image.tsx` — both use `next/og`'s `ImageResponse` to rasterize the same asterism-dot mark against `BRAND_BG_DARK` with `BRAND_FG_ON_DARK` — i.e. **all social/OS-level brand surfaces are permanently dark/Press-colored**, regardless of the user's chosen in-app room. This is a deliberate simplification (favicons/OG images can't read a per-user cookie) and is not something an elvish theme should try to override — a per-user cookie has no meaning to Slack's OG-image scraper or the OS home-screen icon renderer.
- `apps/web/src/app/manifest.ts` — PWA manifest, `background_color`/`theme_color` both `BRAND_BG_DARK`, icons array pointing at `icon.svg` and `/apple-icon`.
- **Verdict**: identity assets (favicon, OG image, manifest, PWA theme-color) are single, brand-level, theme-invariant, and should almost certainly **stay untouched** by an elvish room — they represent the *product*, not the *currently active room*, exactly as they already ignore the Study/Press distinction today. `build-icons.mjs` itself was not found by direct path search this pass (likely named slightly differently or colocated under a different script name) and would need a second look only if the design brief decides identity assets *should* vary by room — which nothing in the current architecture supports and no precedent (Oracle doesn't touch icons either) recommends.

## 5. Where beauty already lives (five most designed corners — taste anchors)

1. **The Press canvas grain** (`globals.css` `body::before`, Two Rooms cutover) — a single SVG `feTurbulence` data-URI, opacity 0.035, applied only in dark room, with real typographic reasoning behind *why* (halation compensation: bigger type, opened tracking, thicker rules, grain for depth). This is the house's best example of "materials before decoration" done with genuine craft — the grain is inseparable from a documented physical-print theory, not applied for vibe alone.
2. **The Black Forest Oracle** (`[data-theme="oracle"]`) — the only fully-realized alternate room: three dedicated self-hosted display faces (EB Garamond, IM Fell English, UniFraktur), a closed nine-token palette, its own marginalia-fg distinct from body-fg. Proof the house *can* and *does* commit to a maximalist, atmospheric alternate register when the content (an oracle/divination feature) earns it.
3. **Editorial ResourceRow** (`ResourceRow.module.css`) — the restraint end of the spectrum: CSS grid, hairline-only separation, no cards, per-room hairline weight fork. Proof the house's default taste is *refusal* — beauty through typographic rhythm and negative space, not chrome.
4. **Machine Hand's attribution rail + colophon** (`machine-hand-hard-cutover.md`) — a tiny, disciplined visual vocabulary (cool ink, hairline rail, small-caps signature) built to solve one honesty problem (never let machine prose pass as human) with minimum visual footprint. Shows the house's preferred move for "a third voice needs its own look" is *subtraction and precision*, not a new decorative system.
5. **The asterism brand mark** (`icon.svg`/`apple-icon.tsx`/`opengraph-image.tsx`) — three dots, one consistent geometry (`ASTERISM_DOTS`/`ASTERISM_VIEWBOX`), reused across every identity surface, recolored only by two brand tokens. Proof the house values one small, endlessly reusable geometric idea over a library of one-off marks — relevant if elvish ever wants its own accent glyph (a single reusable leaf/rune shape, not a gallery of them).

## Open questions for the design-brief phase (not answered here by design)

1. **Global room vs. scoped persona?** Two Rooms (global, fork `--surface-*`/`--ink-*`) vs. Oracle (scoped, own `--oracle-*` namespace, route- or wrapper-gated). Elvish could plausibly be either — a full third settings option, or a special mode/route like Oracle. The docs don't dictate this; it's the single biggest architectural fork for the design brief to resolve.
2. **Where does Tengwar actually render?** As inline SVG rule/border ornaments (safest, matches "unreadable for core UI text" constraint), as a decorative face used only for single ornamental glyphs/dividers (would need a woff2 acquired and self-hosted per the no-CDN-fonts rule), or purely as a background-image accent (cheapest, zero a11y risk). All three are viable; the brief should pick based on how much genuine typographic craft vs. low-risk decoration is wanted.
3. **Does the reader get its own elvish `.readerThemeElvish` block**, given the reader's theme system is documented as deliberately independent of the app room? If yes, that's a second, parallel token surface to design (mirroring how Study/Press required explicit "compose, don't fight" reasoning in Two Rooms §4.5).
